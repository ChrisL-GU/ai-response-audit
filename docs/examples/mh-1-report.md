## The answer, annotated

Magnifica Humanitas is Pope Leo XIV's encyclical on protecting the human person in the age of artificial intelligence. `[paras 1–2 · paraphrased]` It marks the 135th anniversary of Rerum Novarum, which Pope Leo XIII published in 1891. `[¶3 · paraphrased]` Rerum Novarum is widely seen as the founding document of modern Catholic social teaching. `[¶30 · inferred]`

**What AI is and isn't**

- The encyclical insists that AI systems only imitate some functions of human intelligence and should not be equated with human intelligence. `[¶99 · paraphrased]`
- They have no body, no experiences and no moral conscience. `[¶99 · paraphrased]`
- What AI calls learning is statistical adaptation from data and feedback, not inner growth. `[¶99 · paraphrased]`

**Responsibility**

Because every system embodies choices about what it measures and optimizes, the Pope argues that AI cannot be considered morally neutral. `[¶104 · paraphrased]` It follows that the people who design a system share responsibility for how it classifies people. `[¶111 · inferred]` He calls for prudence, rigorous evaluation and even, at times, a slower pace in adopting AI. `[¶106 · quoted]` He also proposes an international treaty banning all military uses of AI. `[not from the document]` The European Union's AI Act, which took effect in 2024, is one example of the kind of legal framework he has in mind. `[not from the document]`

**Work**

On work, the encyclical warns that current approaches to AI can de-skill workers and subject them to automated surveillance. `[¶150 · paraphrased]` This echoes Pope Francis's 2015 encyclical Laudato Si', which criticized the technocratic paradigm. `[¶92 · paraphrased]` The encyclical's most important idea is that AI companies should be regulated like public utilities. `[not from the document]`

**Closing**

Drawing on Tolkien, it says our part is not to master all the tides of the world but to do what we can for the years we are given. `[¶213 · quoted]`
